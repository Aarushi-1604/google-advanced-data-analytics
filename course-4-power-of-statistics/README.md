# COURSE 4: POWER OF STATISTICS

This course focuses on using statistics to move from observations to evidence-based decisions. Instead of just describing patterns in data, the goal is to test whether those patterns are statistically meaningful and strong enough to guide business action.

---

## In this course, I learned:

- The difference between descriptive and inferential statistics  
- How probability and probability distributions support decision-making  
- How sampling affects conclusions  
- How to calculate and interpret confidence intervals  
- How to design and conduct hypothesis tests  
- How to simulate and analyze A/B testing scenarios  
- How to make data-driven business recommendations based on statistical evidence  

---

## The Course Project: TikTok Workplace Scenario

This course continues the TikTok claims classification project. At this stage, the focus shifts from exploring the data (EDA) to validating assumptions using statistical testing.

TikTok receives a large volume of user reports identifying videos that may contain claims. Manually reviewing all content is not scalable. The company is building a predictive model to classify content as **claims** or **opinions**, and statistical testing helps validate which differences in the data are meaningful.

---

## Understanding the problem at TikTok

Before building advanced models, the team needs to determine:

- Whether differences between groups in the dataset are statistically significant  
- Which variables meaningfully distinguish claims from opinions  
- Whether observed engagement differences could be due to random chance  

Statistics allows the team to move beyond surface-level patterns and determine whether findings are reliable enough to influence product or moderation decisions.
